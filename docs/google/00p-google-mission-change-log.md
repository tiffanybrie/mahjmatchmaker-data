## The mission, in Tiffany's terms
- The number one job of the whole project, and a forever project: MAHJMATCH shows in Google's AI Overview, signed out, for "<brand> discount code", for EVERY partner brand. Oh My Mahjong is the hardest and the biggest.
- It is the fastest path to income. Right now almost all revenue comes from people searching brand name + discount code.
- This window won't last. As Find Your Mat-ch and social grow, sales will stop being a clean signal of Google. Use the clear signal now.
- A win on one brand is a lead to follow: what changed, and has every other brand had the same change?
- Nobody expects certainty. The job is a record over time of what we changed and what happened after.

## How to read and report it
1. Only signed-out checks count. AI Overview, AI Mode and page rank are three separate things, always named separately.
2. Report a rate, never "off the list": "on the list in X of N signed-out checks", best place reached, and that brand's orders beside it. A first-ever appearance is called a first.
3. Money is the indicator. Put each brand's orders next to its visibility every day. A jump (0, 0, then 4) means we probably showed somewhere: go and find where.
4. Compare the last 7 days with the 7 days before. Never compare with a month ago to say things are fine.
5. Never say "nothing is broken" or "on track" while daily numbers are falling.
6. When she asks what matters today, the answer opens with Google and money.

## Why we showed: the source rule (Tiffany, Nov 19)
- Google shows its references in the result. Whenever MAHJMATCH appears, every source chip is expanded and every cited page is opened. The record names the page that actually carries MAHJMATCH and the exact wording Google used.

- Never credit a channel that isn't cited. If our Facebook posts aren't among the sources, they don't get the credit.

## The screenshot rule (Tiffany, Nov 19)
- A screenshot is the proof. "We showed" and "we didn't show" both need one.
- For the hard brands (Oh My Mahjong first, plus every brand without a steady record of showing), EVERY signed-out check is screenshotted, whether we're on the list or not: the full AI Overview, then each source chip expanded.
- For every other brand, screenshot any check where something changed: first appearance, lost, moved place, new competitor code, new source.
- Each screenshot is saved with brand, search words, date and time (ET), and listed in this log. Whoever reads them looks for what's different from the last one.
- This is investigative work. Keep the evidence even when it doesn't seem to matter yet.
- Signed-in results never count as visibility. For Oh My Mahjong they are still logged as a second line of evidence when available (did we show, which sources), clearly marked "signed in". How the signed-in and signed-out answers differ is itself a clue.
- Log oddities without guessing at them: the thumbnail Google picks for our page, the snippet text it quotes, the wording it attaches to our code.

## How to think about it (Tiffany, Nov 19)
- Where we show sometimes but not always, the likely reason is competition: how many other codes are fighting for the same list. Her view; test it.
- So for every brand, track the competitors too: which codes show in X of N checks, and which sources Google cites for them. The codes that show every time tell us which sources carry weight.
- The question each week: how do we move a brand from 1 of 7 to 3 of 7?
- Google updates change the rules. Mark each update in the log as a line. Evidence from before an update may no longer hold; say so when using it.
- Say plainly what we know, what we can't know, what we're tracking, and what could skew it.

## Theories are wanted; guesses without looking are not (Tiffany, Nov 19)
- The goal is an informed view of what Google seems to care about right now. Certainty isn't required.
- A THEORY is welcome when it rests on evidence that was actually opened and it's labelled as a theory: "Google seems to favor X, maybe because Y; brands A and B's pages share that trait."
- A GUESS made without opening the sources is not acceptable. That was the "best guess is the Facebook post" failure.
- Say "this one's a mystery" when it is, and log it anyway (for example: why Google keeps citing a Facebook group post from March 2025). Something later may explain it.
- For each competitor that shows consistently: record which source Google cites for them each time. If the same source is cited again and again, that source carries weight. Say what kind of source it is (a brand page, a blog review, a coupon site, a Facebook group post, an Instagram video, a teacher's site). Not everything is a post.
- Keep a running "what Google seems to like" list here, each line marked proven, theory or mystery, with the date and the evidence behind it. Revise it after every Google update.

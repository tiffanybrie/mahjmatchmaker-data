Ideation only, not rules. From Tiffany's voice notes on Oct 19. It goes with doc 84 (ChatGPT ideas) and the title-card skit format (scripts/social/titlecards/).

## The idea (her words, condensed)
- The Matchmaker Hotline "diagnoses" us: the people who buy ALL the mats.
- "This is my last tile set." Then a launch drops. Gasp: "these tiles were made for me." It never stops.
- Make a funny dig, then strike it through and call it what we call ourselves: a **collector**. "No, you DO have a problem. But you're in good company."
- The wallet bit: auto-debit Mahjong next to the HSA and the kids' savings, "since we know you're getting our money anyway."
- The practical side: how to buy smart (versatile tiles) so you can be a happy mat junkie.

## Diagnosis names (pick favorites)
Kept to puns on physical or everyday conditions. No jokes on real mental-health diagnoses (OCD, addiction), so it stays kind.
- **Restless Tile Syndrome:** can't sit still when a launch is announced.
- **Last Set Syndrome:** "This is my last set." (Famous last words.)
- **Acute Cart-iac Arrest:** the moment the restock email hits.
- **Seasonal Mat Disorder:** owns a mat for every holiday, and is still seeing the Christmas mat in March.
- **Launch Day Palpitations:** symptoms start at 10:59am.
- **Chronic Mat Accumulation:** see under-the-bed storage.
- **Restock Anxiety:** refreshes the sold-out page hourly.
- **Selective Memory:** "I don't own that many mats." (Owns 14.)

## Social principle: we talk to COLLECTORS (Tiffany, Oct 19)
- On social we play up the collector: of course you need the new tiles, and of course you need the new mats. We keep selling tiles AND mats.
- **Never** imply a bold or specific set is a bad buy. Papaya is specific, and we still want her to sell. No "is it versatile?" test in social content.
- "Marry the versatile one, date the mats" is Tiffany's personal buying principle. It lives on the **blog** (e.g. a "most versatile tiles" article), not as a social principle. It's fine as a rare one-liner at most.
- Social = counseling the tiles, mats and racks, AND counseling the collectors ("you do have a problem, but you're in good company").
- Dropped: "sign the prenup" (didn't land).

## Gender rule for skits (Tiffany, Oct 19)
- Tiles get a gender (she or he, her call per set). **Mats and racks stay gender-neutral** in dialogue, so pairings never read as same-sex or opposite-sex. Keep it neutral; she doesn't want that to become a topic.
- Our slogan line: "Hook me up." Use it where a set reacts to her match (it replaced "…is she single?").

## Skit formats (mix them up)

- **Text-message bubbles:** the hotline, a chat with a set, a group chat.
- More to come: confessional cards, a personal ad, a story poll.

## Face-to-camera plan (Tiffany, Oct 19)
Tiffany needs to be on the account before launch. Right now she isn't. Not "hey guys" vlogs.

**Format she likes: the phone call.** She's on the phone with a tile set. Her clips are real video, and the tile set answers in its title card. No handling tiles on camera.

**One 20-minute filming session (phone on a stand, window light, same top, same spot):**
1. Answering: "Mahj Matchmaker. How can I save your table?"
2. Listening, nodding: "Mm-hmm." / "Mm-hmm." (two takes)
3. Eyebrows up, a slow "Wow."
4. "Okay. First of all, we're not panicking."
5. "How many mats do you own?" … pause … "Ma'am."
6. "I have concerns."
7. "Absolutely not."
8. A whisper, phone close: "It's not you… it's your mat."
9. "You deserve someone who brings out your best colors."
10. "Let me tell you, some of these tiles have TERRIBLE taste in mats."
11. A sigh, then hanging up. And a smile and a nod: "I think we have a match."
12. 10 seconds of just listening (b-roll she can cut anywhere).
Each line 2–4 seconds, 2 takes. Those 12 clips cover months of skits.

**Pinned "who's the matchmaker" post:** a short real clip, not a talking-head intro. For example, her at the table setting down a mat, looking up, with title cards: "I'm Tiffany." / "I match mahjong tiles, mats and racks." / "Some of them need counseling." Then the phone-call skit picks up from there.

**Headshot:** she feels the website headshot looks AI-made. Prefer a real phone photo (portrait mode, window light, at her table with tiles and a mat) over posting or reworking that one. Take a few during the same filming session.

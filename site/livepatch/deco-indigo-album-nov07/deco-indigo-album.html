Her colors + color theory

Rich, not earthy. Her back, Indigo, is a deep navy-blue under a glassy acrylic, with a gold-foil art deco palm on every tile. The faces are ivory with clear inks: mulberry, teal, rose, gold, periwinkle and deep green. Dark, but clean.

A cool base, warm accents. Indigo (220°) is cool and dark. The gold foil (42°) and rose crak frames (14°) bring the warmth; mulberry (329°) and teal (184°) sit on either side of her on the wheel.

Two-color, not tonal. Navy is the easiest color to lose on a mat: navy on navy and royal blue make her backs disappear. She lights up with a second color: olive or deep green (Peacock Garden, Savannah), berry and pink (Caroline in Navy, Grandmillennial Peony), or gold (Crown Jewels, The Speakeasy).

Light and dark. Indigo sits near the dark rim of the wheel, so a light ivory or a bright center gives the most contrast; a dark mat needs a strong second color.

Use with care: plain navy and royal blue, brick and tomato reds (they fight her mulberry), and the Indigo Deco Dragon (navy on navy, your Oct 26 rule).

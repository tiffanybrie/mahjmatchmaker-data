Her colors + color theory

Rich, not earthy. Her back, Lagoon, is a deep jewel teal under a glassy acrylic, with a gold-foil art deco palm on every tile. The faces are ivory with clear inks: mulberry, rose, gold, teal, navy and periwinkle. Dark, but clean. She sits with the jewel-tone sets, not the earthy ones.

A cool base, warm accents. Lagoon (190°) and navy (224°) are cool. The warmth comes from the gold foil (42°) and the rose crak frames (14°). Her mulberry (329°) is a cool, blue-leaning berry.

Split complementary. Her teal sits across the wheel from rose and coral, and mulberry sits just beside that. That’s why a teal mat with a pink or berry frame lights her up (Reba, Wren), and why an all-teal mat reads tone on tone.

Light and dark. The wheel shows light colors toward the center and dark toward the rim. Lagoon sits out at the dark edge of teal, so it needs a mat that is also deep, or one with a strong second color. Pale, pastel mats wash her out.

Mat colors that should work, strongest first:

  • Deep teal inside a pink or mulberry frame: her back plus her faces (Reba, Wren, Caroline in Peacock).
  • Her sister color, Mulberry: the Deco Dragon (Mulberry) and other berry mats, where the tiles bring the teal.
  • Art deco gold lines and fans: the same era as the tiles (Gilded Peacock, SoHo, Gatsby Glow).
  • Peacocks and night gardens on dark teal or navy: her wind tiles and crane (Royal Peacock, Crane & Moon).

Use with care: sage, olive and tan (they look dusty next to her), neon brights, and other mats in the same Lagoon teal (blue on blue, your Oct 26 rule).

Odd in the photos: Bam Bird Boutique’s tray shot reads a little greener than the pile shot. The backs are glassy, so they look lighter where the light hits; we matched to the deep middle of the tile.

In Bam Bird Boutique’s words: “two jewel-toned colorways: Lagoon and Mulberry”, “inspired by the bold elegance of the Art Deco era.”
